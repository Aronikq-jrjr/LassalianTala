=== DISCIPLINE OFFICE (CRD) AND HOW DISCIPLINE CASES ARE RESOLVED (Handbook 7.6-7.7) ===
7.6 Center for Restorative Discipline
The Center for Restorative Discipline (CRD) promulgates and implements
the restorative approach in handling cases on discipline, behavior-related
incidents, conflict resolutions, and policy-related matters for students.
The CRD is generally tasked to implement student discipline provisions of
the Undergraduate Student Handbook in accordance with the College’s
philosophy of discipline.
The CRD Director formulates and implements the general operations and

programs of the CRD. The deputy officers assist the CRD Director in the
implementation of discipline programs and policies and in the overseeing of
the daily operations of CRD. The discipline officers enforce the disciplinary
rules and regulations of the College. They also assist students through the
CRD formative intervention programs.
CRD provides the following services to students:
A. Process, resolve and document discipline cases/reports utilizing various
alternative dispute resolution strategies.
B. Provide Benilde Discipline Certificate.
C. Administer and enforce the Lost and Found Items Policy and handle
initial storage of lost and found items and subsequent turnover of unclaimed
items.
D. Implement advocacy and orientation programs, such as the Restorative
Discipline Course in BigSky.
E. Process discipline related forms such, as but not limited to, violation,
clearances, and temporary pass.
F. Proactively monitor student behavior and activities on campus, online,
and during school-sanctioned events.
7.7 Resolving Discipline Cases
There are three (3) possible ways of resolving discipline cases presented
to/filed by the CRD 1) resolution by the CRD Director; 2) resolution by the
Discipline Board; and 3) resolution by the Committee on Decorum and
Investigation (CODI).
7.7.1 CRD Director level:
In general, the CRD Director is responsible for resolving and deciding
on discipline cases brought to their attention.
7.7.1.1 Jurisdiction
7.7.1.1.1 Minor Offense as stipulated in Section 7.8.1
7.7.1.1.1.1 Composition
The Center for Restorative Discipline (CRD) Director
or his/her duly deputized representative.
7.7.1.1.1.2 Procedure
Any member of the Benildean community may file
a complaint. In the event that a written complaint
is led, respondents shall be given three (3) working
days within which to file their written response. After
the written response is received, the respondent
shall appear before the CRD Director or their duly
authorized representative for a case conference.
Failure or refusal to appear before the CRD Director
or their duly authorized representative, despite due
notice and without sufficient cause, shall not delay

or suspend the investigation and resolution of the
case.
Regardless of the penalty imposed, students who fail
to comply with the summons or requirements of the
CRD Director or their duly authorized representative
shall not be admitted to class, and/or their academic
records shall be withheld until compliance is met.
If the student is found responsible, the corresponding
sanction shall be imposed. The violation shall also be
recorded in the Violation Form and entered into the
student’s official deportment record.
College Officials and academic personnel shall have
the right to impose appropriate measures in case
of minor offenses or infractions committed in their
presence and shall be properly coordinated with
CRD.
7.7.1.1.1.3 Decision
The Center for Restorative Discipline (CRD) Director
or his/her duly deputized representative shall decide
the matter.
7.7.1.1.1.4 Appeal
The decision of the CRD Director may be appealed
to the Vice President for Lasallian Mission and
Student Life (VP-LMSL). The appeal shall not delay
the execution of the penalty unless the VP-LMSL,
in the exercise of discretion and considering the
gravity of the offense and the circumstances of the
complainant, orders the suspension of the execution
of the penalty until the appealed decision has been
resolved.
Charges shall be expunged from the records, and no
sanction shall be served should the VP-LMSL find the
respondent not responsible.
7.7.1.1.2 Major offenses, where: a) the respondent executes in
writing a voluntary and full admission or confession of guilt;
or b) the corresponding penalty for which does not exceed
seven (7) days of suspension.
As deemed necessary, the CRD Director may elevate the
case to the College Discipline Board, or CODI for further
investigation or resolution. The CRD Director may sit as a
resource person in the Discipline Board, but at no time shall
he/she participate in the deliberation.
Major offenses brought to the attention of the CRD Director,
where the respondent provides an incomplete or qualified

admission or a denial, are elevated to the Discipline Board.
Likewise, discipline cases pertaining to enrollment-related
requirements shall also be endorsed to the Discipline Board
for resolution while complaints related to the Safe Spaces
Act are referred to CODI for investigation and resolution.
7.7.1.1.2.1 Composition
The CRD Director has sole authority to make
decisions regarding any discipline case brought to
his/her attention.
7.7.1.1.2.2 Procedure
A written complaint or report must be submitted
either through email (crd@benilde.edu.ph) or in
person at any CRD Office within five (5) school
days from the date of the incident. However,
this prescriptive period may be extended at the
discretion of the CRD Director for equitable reasons.
In case of circumstances where the identity of
the offender or the commission of the offense
cannot be known despite reasonable action, it
shall be counted within five (5) school days upon
ascertainment of the identity or offense. Written
complaints that fall under Offenses against Persons
and Private Offenses may be required by the CRD
Director to be notarized.
Initial reports received through the Benilde Student
Government must be forwarded within the day
or the next school day to CRD for appropriate
processing and/or endorsement.
Upon receipt of the written complaint or report, the
CRD:
1. Conducts a preliminary investigation
2. A copy of Notice of Complaint along with the
attached written complaint and other related
documents are given to the concerned student/s.
Parents are also furnished with the same copy along
with the Parent Reply Slip.
The student’s refusal to acknowledge the receipt
of the Notice of Complaint and other related
documents will be noted, but it will not delay or stop
the discipline procedure.
3. Requires the student/s concerned to submit
a duly notarized written response within five (5)
school days upon receipt of the notice.
In preparing the response, the student/s may seek

the assistance of a legal counsel, if desired.
Failure of the student(s) to submit a notarized
response will be considered a waiver of their right to
present evidence on their behalf. In such cases, the
complaint will be decided based on the evidence
already on record.
Note: If a Deaf student is involved, a sign language
interpreter shall be provided by the School of
Deaf Education and Applied Studies in all case
proceedings.
4. A case conference will be scheduled between the
CRD Director, the student involved, and the discipline
officer handling the case. Parents have the option to
join the conference. The purpose of the meeting is to
discuss the case and hear the student’s side.
Failure of the student(s) to appear during the case
conference will be considered a waiver of their right
to be heard and present evidence on their behalf. In
such cases, the complaint will be decided based on
the evidence on record.
7.7.1.1.2.3 Decision
The CRD Director will issue a decision on the case
during the case conference. Copies thereof shall be
furnished to all parties concerned.
7.7.1.1.2.4 Appeal
If the respondent(s) find the CRD Director’s decision
unfavorable, they may file an appeal with the
OVPLMSL within five (5) working days upon receiving
the Notice of Resolution. The appeal must be in
writing clearly stating the grounds and providing
supporting evidence. Appeals not filed within the
specified period will render the CRD Director’s
decision final and executory. The OVPLMSL will
decide on the appeal within 10 working days unless
an extension is necessary.
7.7.2 Discipline Board
The College Discipline Board, hereinafter referred to as the Board,
hears and decides cases under its jurisdiction and those endorsed by
the CRD Director. Offenses that may be meted with: (a) a sanction of
suspension beyond two (2) weeks;(b) exclusion; and (c) expulsion are
submitted to the Board.
7.7.2.1 Jurisdiction
The Discipline Board shall exercise jurisdiction over:

Major offenses—the corresponding penalty for which is beyond
20% of the prescribed school days for the term, that is, more
than 14 school days.
Investigations of specific factual matters that are in the nature
of an appeal regarding a decision made by the CRD Director—
this is to determine the accuracy of the appreciation of the
CRD Director of the evidence presented or to receive newly
discovered evidence that had not been available at the time
the matter was under investigation by the CRD Director.
7.7.2.2 Composition
7.7.2.2.1 The Chair of the Board, who is appointed by the
Chancellor, and who must be a member of the Integrated
Bar of the Philippines, presides over all hearings and
deliberations. He/She shall have control over all Board
proceedings and at no time shall his/her presence be
dispensed with. The members of the Board and their
alternates are appointed by the Vice President for
Lasallian Mission and Student Life (VP-LMSL) from
recommendations made by the respective sectors,
which include the administration, faculty, and student
government.
A majority of the members of the Board shall constitute a
quorum. A majority of the quorum shall be necessary for
rendering judgments and imposing appropriate sanctions.
7.7.2.2.2 Benilde Special Counsel
As may be deemed necessary, the VP-LMSL may
recommend to the Chancellor the appointment of a
special counsel to act on legal matters affecting College
interests. The special counsel may be asked to do the
following:
A. File with and prosecute charges in the Board for and
on behalf of the College in cases where the College takes
action against students, with or without formal complaints
filed by the persons aggrieved or victimized or whose
rights may have been directly violated, or by any other
person interested or authorized to file such complaints.
B. Prosecute formal charges filed by the Board by any
interested party or authorized person other than the
College upon its own initiative, where the College
nonetheless deems it necessary to protect and promote
the interests and rights of the College or those of the
complainants.
C. Undertake such other actions as may be deemed
necessary by the Chancellor. At its discretion, the Board

may, however, proceed or continue with the hearing of
a disciplinary case despite the non-designation, nonavailability, or absence of a special counsel. The special
counsel shall not participate in the deliberations of the Board
and shall function only as a legal adviser, safeguarding and
promoting the College’s best interests.
7.7.2.3 Procedure
Formal Hearing before the Discipline Board
There are two ways that the Discipline Board may handle
dispositions for major offenses: 1) through a board hearing; or 2)
through summary proceedings.
7.7.2.3.1 Notice of Hearing
A notice of hearing shall be served to the respondent/s at
least three (3) school days before the scheduled date of
hearing. The respondent/s’ failure to file a duly notarized
response within the prescribed period shall not suspend
proceedings.
The respondent/s refusal to acknowledge the receipt of
the notice and other related documents will be noted but
will not hamper proceedings, neither shall the respondent/s’
absence, despite notifications, be grounds for suspension of
proceedings.
7.7.2.3.2 Discipline Board Hearing
7.7.2.3.2.1 Discipline Board hearings are administrative
procedures that may be summary in nature without
the elaborate rules of evidence and procedures
imposed in a court of law. Cross-examination, thus,
shall not be essential but clarificatory questions may
be raised by the Chairperson and by the members of
the Board. The Board shall ensure that due process is
duly satisfied.
7.7.2.3.2.2 Should the respondent/s fail to appear at the initial
hearing after due notice and without sufficient cause,
the absence will be noted; the hearing shall proceed
ex parte; and the Board shall continue to receive
evidence, if any, for the complainant. In the event
that no additional evidence is submitted, the Board
shall render judgment based on the merits of the
evidence presented and admitted. The respondents
shall be informed in the event that new substantial
evidence, which was not readily available during
the time of investigation, is submitted and shall be
rightfully presented as evidence on their behalf.

7.7.2.3.2.3 As far as practicable, hearings will be held
continually. Any delay shall not be allowed except
for justifiable reasons as may be determined by
the Board. A written request for the postponement
based on justifiable reasons must be led and
approved by the Board Chairperson through
the CRD not less than two (2) days before the
scheduled hearing. Notice in writing to parties shall
be sufficient for this purpose. A written request for
postponement beyond the prescribed period shall
be considered denied, and the parties are required
to be present for the scheduled hearing. The Board
may continue to hear witnesses in the absence of
the complainant, but at no time shall the presence
of the respondent be dispensed with unless the
respondent fails to appear at hearings despite due
notice
7.7.2.3.2.4 During the hearing, the complainant/s and the
respondent/s shall be allowed to present their
respective evidence. The complainant/s shall
present first, followed by the respondent/s.
Witnesses shall testify under oath. The Board shall
proceed to evaluate the evidence and testimonies
after all parties have finished their presentation.
7.7.2.3.2.5 The Board Chairperson shall exercise complete
control over the proceedings at all stages, using
every reasonable means to ascertain the facts
as expeditiously and objectively as possible and
without regard to technicalities of law or procedure
but always with regard to due process.
7.7.2.3.2.6 Should a respondent admit to the charges, they
shall be required to execute a written confession
of guilt under oath and appear before the
Board to affirm the admission. In such cases,
formal proceedings may be dispensed with
and the Board will decide on the appropriate
sanctions, which will be enforced immediately.
The respondent’s voluntary admission of guilt
and request for the discontinuance of the formal
proceedings will be noted on the case records.
7.7.2.3.2.7 Regardless of the penalty imposed, the respondent
shall not be admitted to class nor allowed to
enroll or graduate without complying with the
requirements of the Board/CRD.
Note: Provisions for Audio Recording (CRD Manual)

7.7.2.4 Summary Action by the Board
Should the respondent/s execute a written admission or
confession of guilt, the Board may summarily act upon the case
at hand. Whereupon, the hearing will be terminated as soon
as the respondent gives his/her side and the members of the
Board are through with their clarificatory questions. Any penalty
imposed in summary proceeding is final and immediately
executory unless the Board defers the imposition of the penalty
for meritorious reasons.
7.7.2.5 Decisions
The Board will render a decision within ten (10) school days
or within a reasonable time from the last hearing date. The
written decision will state the facts and reasons for arriving at
such a decision. Copies thereof shall be furnished to all parties
concerned (person responsible, person harmed), the Chancellor,
the Vice President for Lasallian Mission and Student Life and
the Registrar (in enrollment related penalty). Copies of the
decision and the complaint shall be included in the respondent/s’
individual folio and shall constitute part of their record.
7.7.2.6 Appeals
Should the respondent/s find the decision of the Board
unfavorable, s/he may file an appeal with the Office of the
Chancellor within five (5) working days of receipt of the decision.
The appeal must state the grounds and reasons for the appeal.
Appeals not led within the indicated period will render the
decision of the Board final and executory.
The Office of the Chancellor will decide on the appeal within 10
working days unless an extension is necessary.
The decision of the Chancellor, when reviewing a case under this
section, is final and executory.
7.7.3 Committee on Decorum and Investigation
The Committee on Decorum and Investigation (CODI) will serve
as an independent internal grievance mechanism. It is the main
body responsible for investigating and resolving cases of GenderBased Sexual Harassment (GBSH) in the workplace, as well as in
educational and training institutions.
7.7.3.1 Jurisdiction
All received complaints regarding violation of Safe Spaces Act
or Republic Act No. 11313.

7.7.3.2 Composition
The Committee on Decorum and Investigation (CODI) shall
be chaired by the GAD Focal Chairperson and composed of
permanent and alternate representatives from the following
sectors: the school administration or management, the
Faculty Association (FA) or faculty members, the Benilde
Student Government (BeSG) the People and Organization
Development Office (PODO) representing Supervisory Rank
Employees, and the Benilde Administrative and Support
Personnel Association (BASPA) representing associates and
rank-and-file employees.
Every CODI shall be headed by a woman and no less than half
of its members shall be women.
7.7.3.3 Procedures
A written complaint must be submitted to the OVPLMSL, PODO
or CRD. Such complaints shall contain the following information:
(1) specific act/s allegedly committed by the perpetrator;
(2) the date, time, place, and witnesses (if any); and (3) such
other proof as may be necessary to support the complainant’s
allegation. Complaints course through the concerned
administrator, the latter shall transmit the report to OVPLMS
within three (3) working days upon the receipt.
If a student, associate, or partner at DLS-CSB does not wish
to file a complaint or request action, but the school becomes
aware of a possible or impending act of Gender-Based
Sexual Harassment (GBSH) or sexual violence, the school
must still investigate the situation. The investigation should
determine the facts and circumstances of the alleged act
and take appropriate steps to resolve the issue. If the college
knows or should reasonably know about such acts that create
a hostile environment, it must act immediately to eliminate
the issue, prevent recurrence, and address its effects. This
includes conducting an investigation and referring the affected
individual for necessary psychosocial or medical services.
The OVPLMSL shall convene the Committee on Decorum and
Investigation (CODI) and forward the complaint to CODI within
48 hours from receipt of the complaint.
The Committee on Decorum and Investigation (CODI) shall
address Gender-Based Sexual Harassment (GBSH) and online
sexual harassment following the rules outlined in the College’s
manuals and handbooks, as long as they comply with legal
requirements. CODI shall, at all times, uphold due process,
investigate, and make decisions on written complaints within
ten (10) working days, provided the necessary documentation
is complete. Documents, responses, and evidence must be
submitted within five (5) working days; otherwise, the right to

respond is waived, and the case will be resolved based on the
available documents.
7.7.3.4 Decision
The CODI will issue a decision within ten (10) school days or
within a reasonable period after the final hearing. The written
decision will include the facts of the case and the reasons behind
the ruling.
7.7.3.5 Appeal
Should the respondent/s find the decision of the CODI
unfavorable, s/he may file an appeal with the Office of the
Chancellor within five (5) working days of receipt of the decision.
The appeal must state the grounds and reasons for the appeal.
Appeals not led within the indicated period will render the
decision of the Board final and executory.
The Office of the Chancellor will decide on the appeal within 10
working days unless an extension is necessary.
The decision of the Chancellor, when reviewing a case under this
section, is final and executory.

=== DISCIPLINARY SANCTIONS (Handbook 7.9.1-7.9.4) ===
7.9 Disciplinary Sanctions
The penalty to be imposed for each offense shall depend on the nature
and/or gravity of the violation. A just and reasonable disciplinary action
shall be rendered for any offense or violation. No cruel or physically harmful
punishment, however, shall be imposed or applied against any student. It
is further provided that no disciplinary sanction shall be applied upon any
student except for cause as defined in this Student Handbook or in the
Manual of Regulations for Private Schools, and only after due process shall
have been observed.
7.9.1 Sanctions for Major Offenses
Any or a combination of the following disciplinary actions for major
offenses may be imposed on the student:
7.9.1.1 Loss of privileges, such as loss of scholarship/ student aid.
7.9.1.2 Discipline probation, where the student is required to adhere to
the provisions of the Discipline Probation Contract.
7.9.1.3 Holding of records (that is, the student will not be allowed
access to them).

7.9.1.4 Revocation of degree previously conferred; non- conferment
of degree; deferral or withholding of degree, diploma, and/or
certificate.
7.9.1.5 Nullification or the cancellation and invalidation of enrollment in
the College.
7.9.1.6 The three (3) categories of disciplinary administrative sanctions
for serious offenses or violation of school rules and regulations
that may be applied upon erring students are: suspension,
exclusion, and expulsion.
Article XIV, section 77 of the Manual of Regulations for Private
Schools delineate the “Categories of Administrative Penalties”,
thus:
7.9.1.6.1 Suspension
Suspension is a penalty in which the school is allowed to
deny or deprive erring students of attendance in classes
for a period not exceeding 20% of the prescribed class days
for the academic year or term.
Students under disciplinary suspension are considered on
forced leave status and are automatically dropped from
the enrollment list.
The decision of the school on every case involving the
penalty of suspension which exceeds 20% of the prescribed
school days for a school year or term shall be forwarded
to the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) Regional
Office concerned within 10 days from the termination of the
investigation of each case for its information.
A student under investigation in a case involving the penalty
of expulsion may be placed under preventive suspension
and barred from entering the school premises if there
is sufficient basis to believe that the continued stay of the
student during the period of the investigation constitutes a
distraction to the normal operation of the school or poses
a risk or danger to the life or persons and property in the
school. (See Section 7.9.3)
7.9.1.6.2 Exclusion
Exclusion is a penalty in which the school is allowed to
exclude or drop the name of the erring student from
the school rolls for being undesirable, in which case
transfer credentials are immediately issued. A summary
investigation shall have been conducted, and no prior
approval by the CHED is required in the imposition of the
penalty. The decision of the school on every case involving
the penalty of exclusion from the rolls together with all the

pertinent papers shall, therefore, be led in the school for
a period of one year to afford the CHED the opportunity
to review the case in the event an appeal is taken by the
party concerned.
7.9.1.6.3 Expulsion
Expulsion is an extreme penalty on erring students
consisting of their exclusion from admission to any public
or private school in the Philippines. This requires the
prior approval of the CHED Chairperson. The penalty
may be imposed for acts or offenses constituting gross
misconduct, dishonesty, hazing, carrying deadly weapons,
immorality, and selling and/or possession of prohibited
drugs such as marijuana. It is also imposed on students
exhibiting drug dependency, and for drunkenness,
hooliganism, vandalism, forging or tampering with school
records or school forms, and other serious school offenses
such as assaulting other students or school personnel,
instigating illegal strikes, or similar concerted actions
resulting in the disruption of activities. Any other acts that
cause the stoppage of classes or that prevent any other
student or school personnel from entering the school
premises and attending classes or discharging their duties
will also warrant expulsion. The decision of the school on
every case involving the penalty of expulsion together
with the supporting papers shall be forwarded to the
CHED Regional Office concerned within 10 days of the
termination of the investigation of each case.
7.9.1.7 Other Penalties
Any of the above sanctions, except expulsion or exclusion,
may require a student to undergo the Center for Restorative
Discipline Formation Program; render in-school service; or be
imposed sanctions, which shall be determined by the Discipline
Board, or the CRD Director, as the case may be.
7.9.1.8 Other consequences that may be meted out for major
offenses that may be considered less serious:
A. Participation in projects or activities that aim to promote
discipline on campus.
B. In-school service, defined as a designated number of hours
of volunteer or service/work to the College( a student may
be offered an opportunity to complete a specified number of
hours of work in lieu of other sanctions).
C. Compliance with educational requirements such as
completion of specific seminars/ workshops required by CRD.
D. Any other sanctions that may be deemed appropriate by

the CRD Director/Board, whichever may decide the case.
7.9.2 Sanctions for Minor Offenses
Any or a combination of the following actions may be imposed on
the student for minor offenses:
A. Verbal or written admonition.
B. Submission of a letter of apology and/or a written explanation to
all parties concerned.
C. Rendering of service to the College.
D. Attendance in seminars/workshops relevant to the specific
violation.
E. Any other sanctions that may be deemed appropriate by the
CRD Director/Discipline Board, whichever may decide the case.
7.9.3 Preventive Suspension
7.9.3.1 The VP-LMSL is granted the special power to suspend, for
a maximum of fifteen (15) days, effective immediately upon
imposition students apprehended or caught in the act or
positively identified as having directly or indirectly participated in
cases of:
A. Fistfights, brawls, direct assaults, or any other kind of physical
confrontation with or without arms, whether or not fraternity- or
sorority-related, on/off campus.
B. Organizing boycotts or student strikes using force, coercion,
threats, or intimidation to gather participants.
C. In circumstances when continued presence on campus of the
respondent poses a potential risk to the integrity of the case
investigation or may constitute a continued threat, intimidation,
or undue influence to the complainant, witnesses, or other
members of the community.
D. Other similar circumstances or offenses where the continued
presence of the offender would disrupt the normal operations
of the College, pose a threat to the safety of the College
community or any of its members, or cause destruction to
College property
7.9.3.2 Respondents affected by the decision of the VP-LMSL under
Section 7.9.1.6.1 may file with the Office of the Chancellor a
motion for reconsideration within a period of five (5) working
days of its receipt. The motion shall not delay the execution
of the penalty unless the Chancellor—in the exercise of
discretion and in considering the gravity of the offenses and the
circumstances of the case as well as that of the complainant
or respondent—orders the suspension of the execution of the
penalty until the appealed decision has been resolved.

7.9.3.3 If no motion for reconsideration is led, the decision shall become
final and executory. Decisions by the Chancellor on the motion
for reconsideration shall also be final and not open to appeals.
7.9.3.4 The Chancellor’s actions under Section 7.9.3.2 and the exercise
of such special powers provided for under Section 7.9.5.1 shall
not prejudice other disciplinary actions that may be brought
against the same student/s for the same incident.
7.9.4 Teacher’s Prerogative on Cases of Cheating
7.9.4.1 Students caught cheating on major exams (midterms and
finals) and/or major academic requirements may be given
a grade of R by the faculty concerned, as provided under
Section 7.8.2.45.
Students caught cheating on quizzes, seat works, and other
academic activities may be given an outright failing mark on
the particular activity.
In both cases mentioned above, the following must be
observed:
A. The faculty concerned secures a written admission from
the student concerned. SDEAS students may submit their
admission through video and transcription.
B. The faculty concerned filed a report to the Center for
Restorative Discipline for documentation and reference
purposes within five (5) school days of knowing or witnessing
the incident.
7.9.4.2 If the student denies the cheating allegation, the faculty may
file a written complaint to CRD together with the material
evidence or written testimony of witnesses, within five (5)
school days of the knowing or witnessing the incident, if
possible.
7.9.4.3 The student concerned shall at all times be granted the right to
answer the charges against him in writing and have access
to evidence therein.
7.9.4.4 The CRD shall determine if the complaint filed should be
decided upon summarily by the CRD Director or be endorsed
for further investigation to the Discipline Board. The faculty
concerned shall then wait for further advice from CRD.
7.9.4.5 If the act of cheating on any academic requirements is
repeated and was admitted, CRD will file the complaint
accordingly.
7.9.4.6 A student who has been found responsible for academic
dishonesty is disqualified from graduating with honors.
